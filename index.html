Where every number comes from

  1. River flow forecast: Open-Meteo Flood API (GloFAS v4), open-meteo.com, CC BY 4.0. Daily river_discharge (m³/s) for today and the next 2 days (Europe/Dublin). Read live every time.
  2. Rain warnings: Met Éireann warning_IRELAND.json, CC BY 4.0. Counties come as codes in regions (e.g. Cork = EI04, Roscommon = EI24). Read live every time.
  3. Team Sheet: River Watch Team Sheet (Google Sheets, read live every time).
  4. High and flood marks: history-summary.json, built once from the same API, daily flow 1 Jan 2016 – 31 Dec 2025 (3,653 days per town). High mark = 95th percentile (only 5% of days were higher). Flood mark = 99th percentile (only 1% of days were higher).
  5. River check: each town's model point was checked against its official OPW gauge on the named river (waterlevel.ie, Office of Public Works); every point is within 4 km of the gauge and the flows grow downstream as they should.
  6. AI: Anthropic Claude Haiku 5.5 explains the plan. The levels, crews and counts are worked out by code, not by the AI.

The rules used (Flood Plan 2026/27)

  • Highest flow in the next 3 days below the high mark → Normal. At or above → Standby. At or above the flood mark → Barriers.
  • Orange or red rain warning for the town's county in the next 48 hours → one level up. Yellow changes nothing. Athlone and Carrick-on-Shannon also count Roscommon.
  • Practice ("rehearsal") values in the Sheet replace the forecast or warning, and are always labelled.
  • Three crews (Clonmel, Mallow, Athlone), two towns each, own area only. Most over the flood mark (in %) goes first. No barrier set or no crew place → standby measures and a flag to the Area Manager.
